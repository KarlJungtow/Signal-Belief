This study is composed of three parts. In each part you participate in 12 rounds of decision-making. In each round, you will be asked to make two choices. We refer to these as “Consumption choice” and “Price belief”. Both of these choices may affect your final payoff. The specific instruction for each part will be given to you at the beginning of each part.

Consumption choice

Each round represents two time periods, called Period 1 and Period 2. In each round, you will receive an endowment of virtual goods in both periods. We denote the endowment in Period 1 by E1 and the endowment in Period 2 by E2.

These endowments will be either

  • E1 = 15 and E2 = 5

or

  • E1 = 5 and E2 = 15

In 6 rounds, you will get the first endowment profile, and in the other 6 rounds, you will get the second endowment profile. However, the order in which these profiles appear is random.

Your task is to choose how much to consume in Period 1. We denote this consumption choice by c1. Based on your Period 1 consumption choice, your consumption in Period 2 (denoted c2) is automatically determined. The latter depends on the price in Period 2.

The price of consumption goods in Period 1 is fixed at p1 = 1.

The price of consumption goods in Period 2, p2, is randomly determined. It will be either:

  • 0.5 (50% lower than p1), or
  • 2 (100% higher than p1).

The two possible price levels for p2 have an equal probability (50% chance).

Consumption payoff

Your consumption payoff points earned each round are calculated with a payoff function (shown on screen during the experiment).

You will be given tables showing the possible payoff points you can earn for various integer c1 choices, depending on the endowment profile you receive and on the Period 2 price, p2.

You can also insert numbers with up to one decimal in the box below the table. The associated payoff points will be shown to you in the same line.

Saving and Borrowing

You can save or borrow using money. Your Period 1 saving or borrowing is determined by how your consumption choice c1 compares to your Period 1 endowment E1.

If you consume fewer units than your Period 1 endowment (c1 < E1), you save E1 − c1 units of money, that you can carry over to Period 2. You may think of this as selling E1 − c1 units of the good at price p1. These savings will be used to buy additional consumption goods in Period 2 at the price p2. Then, your Period 2 consumption will be:

c2 = E2 + (E1 − c1) · p1 / p2

If you consume more units than your Period 1 endowment (c1 > E1), you borrow c1 − E1 units of money, that you must repay in Period 2. You may think of this as buying c1 − E1 units of the good at price p1. These borrowings will be repaid by selling a part of your endowment in Period 2 at the price p2. Then, your Period 2 consumption will be:

c2 = E2 − (c1 − E1) · p1 / p2

There is no interest earned on saving, nor is there any interest charged on borrowing.

Constraints on your consumption choice

Your consumption choice is restricted to be in the range:

1 ≤ c1 ≤ E1 + E2

This restriction makes sure that you consume at least 1 unit of the good in each period even if you have to repay your borrowing and that you will always be able to repay your borrowing whatever the price in Period 2.

Price belief

In each round you will receive a hint for the randomly chosen price level for Period 2. The timing, when you receive this hint, differs between the three parts.

This hint consists of a graph containing 400 red and blue dots. The figure below shows an example. Every round, there will be a different hint graph.

Figure: Example hint.

  • If a majority of the dots (> 200) are colored blue, the price level is low: p2 = 0.5.
  • If a majority of the dots (> 200) are colored red, the price level is high: p2 = 2.

The number of blue and red colored dots will never be equal. When you click the “Get hint” button, you will only have 8 seconds to see this graph. After you saw the graph, you will be asked to state your belief about whether the majority of dots is red.

You can state your belief using a slider or type the probability that you attribute to the majority of dots being red. This probability is denoted by q. Selecting 100% would indicate that you are certain that more than half of the dots are red. Selecting 0% would indicate that you are certain that more than half of the dots are blue. However, if you are uncertain, you may state any probability between 0% and 100%.

Belief payoff

You can earn a bonus payoff of 2 Euros that is determined by how close your stated probability is to the actual outcome. More specifically:

  • You will receive 2 Euros with probability q if the majority of dots was red.
  • If the majority of dots was blue, you receive 2 Euros with the probability 1 − q.

This rule makes sure that it is in your own interest to state your true belief.

Final payoff

At the end of the experiment, one round in each part will be randomly selected and your consumption payoff points from these rounds will determine your final monetary payoff. We will transform payoff points to euros with a 1 to 20 ratio (100 points = 5 euros). Another round in each part will be randomly selected for the reward on your stated belief.

For both chosen rounds, we will remind you of your decisions and show you the actual outcomes. For the consumption choice, we will show you the actual Period 2 price level and the computer will calculate your resulting payoff points. For beliefs, the computer will run a lottery to determine whether you earned the 2 Euros bonus using your stated probability. You will only see the actual outcomes for these randomly chosen rounds.

Training rounds

On the next screen, we will ask you a few questions about these instructions to ensure you understand them. Then, there will be two training rounds. Your decisions during these rounds will not affect your final payoff. These rounds are designed to familiarize you with tasks. We will provide feedback on your decisions during these training rounds.
